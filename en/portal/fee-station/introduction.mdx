## What is Fee Station

On different blockchains, transactions require different native tokens (gas tokens like ETH, TRX, or SOL) to pay for gas fees. This means you need to prepare and manage gas balances for each chain. If any address runs out of gas, transactions may fail.

Meanwhile, you might need to settle various types of fees across Cobo Portal, such as service fees and custody fees. These are often handled via emails, spreadsheets, and manual reconciliation—making the process inefficient and error-prone.

Fee Station helps you simplify this. It is a one-stop fee management platform on Cobo Portal. After Fee Station is enabled, funded, and configured for the chain or request, it can cover on-chain gas fees for supported [MPC Wallets](/en/portal/mpc-wallets/introduction) or [Web3 Wallets](/en/portal/custodial-wallets/web3-wallets/introduction) transactions in your organization based on the strategy you configure, and also settle various types of fees with a single balance.

<Note>Currently, Fee Station supports paying gas fees for transactions made with MPC Wallets and Web3 Wallets on EVM-compatible chains, TRON, Solana, and Tempo.</Note>

## Supported wallet types

Fee Station gas fee payment is available for MPC Wallets and Web3 Wallets only.

In the Cobo wallet taxonomy, Web3 Wallets are a subtype of Custodial Wallets. Custodial Wallets also include Asset Wallets, which are not eligible for gas fee payment. Asset Wallets can use the Fee Station balance to settle custody fees only.

| Wallet type | Gas fee payment | Other fees covered |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [MPC Wallets](/en/portal/mpc-wallets/introduction) | Supported | Service fees, custody fees |
| [Custodial Wallets (Web3 Wallets)](/en/portal/custodial-wallets/web3-wallets/introduction) | Supported | Service fees, custody fees |
| [Custodial Wallets (Asset Wallets)](/en/portal/custodial-wallets/asset-wallets/introduction) | Not supported | Custody fees |

## Who should use Fee Station

Fee Station is ideal for any team looking to simplify on-chain fee management, especially:

- **Exchanges and wallet service providers**: Centrally manage and prepay gas fees for end-user transactions, reducing operational overhead.
- **Payment service providers**: Use flat-rate or unified billing models to abstract away blockchain complexity and deliver smoother user experiences.
- **Enterprise developers using Cobo wallets**: Automate fee budgeting, track spending in real time, and receive Webhook alerts for transaction status and balance changes.

## What Fee Station can do for you

### Automatic gas fee handling

When Fee Station is enabled, funded, and configured for a supported chain or request, it can cover gas fees for transactions initiated from MPC Wallets and Web3 Wallets. You can choose to cover fees only when the wallet balance is insufficient, or to always cover them. Once configured, the system checks the condition and processes the gas fee without manual input. For more details about the chain support, please refer to [Supported chains for gas fee payment](#supported-chains-for-gas-fee-payment).

### How Auto Fueling works

To make sure a withdrawal address can pay its own on-chain fees, Fee Station may send a small amount of the native gas token (for example, on the order of `0.001` of the native token) to the address before processing the transaction. The cost of this top-up is deducted from your Fee Station balance. Depending on your configuration and available balances, the deduction may be settled from your USD or USD stablecoin balance even when a native gas token balance is present.

### Unified fee settlement

In addition to covering gas fees, your Fee Station balance can also be used to settle all platform-related charges within Cobo Portal, including service fees and custody fees. You don't need to maintain separate balances for each fee type. A single balance simplifies the billing process and gives you a clear, consolidated view of your spending.

<Tip>
Fee Station supports the following types of fees:
- [Custodial Wallets (Asset Wallets)](/en/portal/custodial-wallets/asset-wallets/introduction): Custody fees
- [MPC Wallets](/en/portal/mpc-wallets/ocw/introduction) / [Custodial Wallets (Web3 Wallets)](/en/portal/custodial-wallets/web3-wallets/introduction): Gas fees, service fees, and custody fees
</Tip>

### Flexible deposit options

You can deposit gas tokens directly, or simply deposit USD or USD stablecoins (such as USDT or USDC) as a unified balance. For transfers that Fee Station covers, it can settle the fee from this balance according to your payment preferences, so you do not need to prepare a separate gas token balance for each chain. For more details about supported gas token or stablecoin types, please refer to [Supported token types](#supported-token-types).

### TRON gas fee optimization with energy rental

When Fee Station is enabled, funded, and configured for TRON, it can use energy on the TRON network and prioritize energy over burning TRX for gas fees. For transfers that Fee Station covers, it handles eligible energy use according to the configured strategy, so you do not need to rent energy yourself.

If Fee Station does not cover a transfer, energy must come from the sending address itself, such as energy the address already holds or has been delegated, or the address must hold TRX to pay the fee. See [Pay gas without Fee Station](#pay-gas-without-fee-station).


### API and webhook integration

Fee Station offers standardized APIs and webhook support, allowing your system to access real-time fee data and receive timely notifications. This makes monitoring and operations tracking easier and more efficient.

For more details, please refer to the [Fee Station API](https://www.cobo.com/developers/v2/api-references/feestation/get-fee-station-transaction-information) and [Webhook documentation](https://www.cobo.com/developers/v2/guides/webhooks-callbacks/webhook-event-type#webhook-event-types).

## Pay gas without Fee Station

You do not have to use Fee Station. If you disable it, or if a transfer is not covered by it, the sending address pays the on-chain fee itself. In that case the sending address must hold the native gas token of the chain it transacts on.

For TRON USDT transfers, the sending address needs available energy or TRX. If energy is insufficient, TRX is burned to pay the fee, so an address holding only TRON USDT and no TRX cannot send.

<Note>When using the API, set `auto_fuel` to `DisableAutoFuel` so Fee Station is not used for the transaction fee under any circumstances. `ProActiveAutoFuel` always uses Fee Station, and `PassiveAutoFuel` uses it only when the source address balance is insufficient. If you omit `auto_fuel`, the transfer behaves as `UsePortalPreference` and follows your saved Portal preference. TRON and Solana do not support `PassiveAutoFuel` because of their fee delegation mechanism.</Note>

## Who pays the on-chain fee

Which side pays depends on whether Fee Station is enabled, funded, and applicable to the transfer:

| Situation | Who pays the fee |
| --- | --- |
| Fee Station is not enabled, or `auto_fuel` is `DisableAutoFuel` | The sending address, from its own gas token balance |
| Fee Station is enabled and funded, and the transfer is eligible | Fee Station, from your Fee Station balance |
| Fee Station is enabled but its balance is not sufficient for the fee | Neither — the transfer is rejected with an insufficient fee token balance error |
| The transfer is on TRON and energy is available | Energy is used, which avoids burning TRX |
| The transfer is on TRON and no energy can be applied | TRX is burned to pay the fee |

Eligibility depends on the chain, the wallet type, and the payment preference that applies to the sending address. See [How to use Fee Station](/en/portal/fee-station/set-up) for how to set and inspect preferences.

## Benefits of using Fee Station

The table below shows the differences between using Fee Station and not using it, helping you understand the benefits of using Fee Station:

| Category            | Without Fee Station                                    | With Fee Station                                         |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Gas preparation**     | Manually prepare gas tokens for each individual blockchain | Deposit gas tokens or USD stablecoins once; when Fee Station is enabled and funded for the chain, it covers gas fees without further manual preparation. |
| **Gas fee strategy**    | Manual intervention required                               | When enabled and configured, applies the saved rules, covering fees only when the wallet balance is insufficient or always covering them, for supported chains and wallet types. |
| **Fee settlement**      | Separate records across multiple chains and tokens         | All fees settled through a single account and in the same token, easier to track   |
| **Failed transactions** | High risk of failure due to insufficient gas               | When enabled, funded, and supported for the transfer, detects insufficient gas and covers the fee, reducing failure risk. |
| **Maintenance cost**    | Frequent manual operations, time-consuming                 | One-time setup enables ongoing automation with less operational effort |
| **Data tracking**       | No unified record of fee activity                          | Exportable and well-organized records of deposits and spending |
